# Political incentives and deferred-adjustment hypothesis

Status: TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS, NOT AN ESTABLISHED UNIVERSAL LAW

## Claims to test
H1: Election and office-holding incentives correlate with policy support before elections or term endings.
H2: Policy support sometimes shifts recession or drawdown timing rather than eliminating vulnerability.
H3: Repeated interventions can increase leverage, valuation gaps, debt-service burdens or future adjustment costs.
H4 (strong falsifiable claim): Every apparent soft landing is merely deferred recession. Do not adopt H4 as a fact; seek counterexamples and identify independent new shocks.

## Causal identification
- Distinguish elected fiscal policymakers from institutionally independent monetary authorities.
- Record administration, congressional control, election dates, fiscal measures, policy rates, balance sheet and credit conditions.
- Record macroeconomic shocks, geopolitical events and financial regulation changes as confounders.
- Compare pre-election with non-election periods; account for policy lags, recession timing, global spillovers and inherited conditions.
- Study whether imbalances persist or resolve, not merely whether a recession occurs at any later date.
- Separate recession outcomes from equity-market 20% drawdown outcomes.
- Analyze cumulative support and vulnerability persistence across 12/24/36/60/84 months.
- Use predeclared event definitions, contemporaneous vintages, complete non-event panels and chronological out-of-sample evaluation.

## Competing explanations
A: genuine adjustment and resolution without recession.
B: intervention defers a contraction while vulnerabilities persist.
C: later contraction reflects a new shock or different endogenous mechanism.
D: intervention changes severity or timing but cannot be identified as sole cause.

## Guardrails
Do not infer political intent from timing alone; use contemporaneous documented statements or credible evidence. Do not attribute all central-bank decisions to elected officials. Do not claim no soft landings exist unless evidence rejects the competing explanations. Do not publish deterministic crash probabilities from selected retrospective events.

## First implementation milestone
Create a structured historical intervention registry with administration, election proximity, policy instrument, announcement/implementation dates, contemporaneous rationale, measured vulnerability changes, subsequent outcome, and evidence-quality rating. Join to the endogenous crash and recovery cohorts after independent source checks.
