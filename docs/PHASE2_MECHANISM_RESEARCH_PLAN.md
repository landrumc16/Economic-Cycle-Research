# Phase 2 — Mechanism-first crash comparison (implementation contract)

## Goal
Determine which economic and financial conditions distinguish crash *mechanisms*, rather than fit a single universal crash template. This document authorizes exploratory retrospective comparisons, not historical predictive accuracy claims.

## Mechanism families (non-exclusive)
| Mechanism | Candidate precursors | Distinguishing question |
|---|---|---|
| Credit/banking | Credit spreads, delinquency, lending standards, leverage, yield curve | Was funding or balance-sheet stress already rising? |
| Inflation/policy tightening | CPI trend, policy rate, real rates, yield curve, energy shocks | Was disinflation policy or supply inflation constraining activity? |
| Equity valuation/speculation | Valuation measures, margin debt where reliable, concentration, equity returns | Did repricing precede broad economic deterioration? |
| External shock | Event timeline, mobility/production interruption, commodity disruptions | Did an identifiable external event cause an abrupt regime break? |
| Sovereign/liquidity | Treasury funding stress, dollar funding, credit spreads | Was a market plumbing or sovereign confidence channel dominant? |

These are hypotheses; an event may receive several mechanism labels with confidence and contradictory evidence. Never equate the event's post-crash consequences with its pre-crash causes.

## Initial event set
1973–75 stagflation, 1980–82 inflation tightening, 2000–02 dot-com, 2007–09 global financial crisis, and 2020 pandemic. These windows are exploratory and not a representative sample of all crashes.

## Next build increments
1. **Baseline retrospective profiles (already running):** year-over-year CPI, year-over-year real GDP, and unemployment increase. Report observation frequency and coverage; do not average monthly and quarterly observations as if interchangeable.
2. **Leading indicator registry:** for each proposed series record source ID, unit, frequency, earliest date, vintage availability, publication lag, transformation, economic mechanism, and data-quality limitations. No series joins until source and units are checked.
3. **Event-aligned panel:** define anchor date for each crash independently of macro outcomes; compute pre-event windows of 3/6/12/24 months, during-event, and recovery; use last *actually available* observation for any PIT analysis.
4. **Comparisons:** distinguish leading conditions, trigger, transmission, policy response, and aftermath. Report both matches and counterexamples; avoid a universal crash probability from five examples.
5. **Cycle and social context:** treat generational/fourth-turning categories as hypotheses, not empirical causes. Use independently measured demographics, sentiment, trust, and political/economic shocks; test incremental explanatory power against economic controls.
6. **Current-economy assessment:** show indicator-specific regimes, disagreement and uncertainty. No backtested predictive score or real-time claim until publisher vintage/release gates are approved.

## Mandatory safeguards
- Current-revised observations are retrospective; ALFRED/BLS/BEA release evidence and availability timestamps must be authenticated before historical signal claims.
- Historical CPI/GDP figures must not be relabeled as a custom universal metric without a documented, stable definition and reproducible calculation.
- Avoid selection bias, data leakage, post-event labeling as a predictor, and using the same events to design and validate a classifier.
- Canonical Google Sheets inputs and promotion gate remain unchanged pending human review.

## Completion checkpoint
Phase 2's first research deliverable is a reproducible event-aligned comparison with explicit leading/trigger/aftermath fields and a data dictionary; then add series incrementally based on mechanism coverage rather than broad unbounded collection.
