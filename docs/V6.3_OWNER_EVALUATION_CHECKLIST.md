# V6.3 Owner Evaluation Checklist

**Purpose:** a structured walk-through of the deployed V6.3 site, so the owner can judge the real product and collect improvements for the next substantial development assignments.
**Release type:** limited internal evaluation release, descriptive economic monitoring. Not a commercial launch.
**Site:** https://landrumc16.github.io/Economic-Cycle-Research/

For each item, record **OK / Improve / Problem** and a short note. Group the "Improve" items afterwards into development assignments. Small wording issues are usually best collected rather than fixed one at a time.

## 0. Release identity (first 2 minutes)
- [ ] `v62_release_manifest.json` shows `build_commit` equal to the V6.3 merge commit, `promotion_outcome: NEW_QUALIFIED`, and an assessment date matching the latest monthly run. (The manifest filename and `version: 6.2` field are kept for publisher compatibility.)
- [ ] The homepage footer disclaimer is present ("not personalized financial advice").

## 1. Homepage — "Where we are / where we're heading / where to look"
- [ ] **Q1 Where is the economy right now?** The condition (Expanding / Slowing / Deteriorating / Mixed) is clear to a non-economist within ~10 seconds.
- [ ] "Why we think this" bullets are understandable and each shows a value and an observation month.
- [ ] "What argues against it" is visible and fair (currently: inflation above goal).
- [ ] "Evidence gaps" explains the missing October 2025 data without alarming or confusing the reader.
- [ ] "Confidence in evidence coverage: Moderate" reads as evidence quality, not as a probability.
- [ ] **Q2 Where do we think it is going?** The direction (currently Firming) and its caveat "treat it as a description, not a signal" are understandable. Decide whether the wording is too hedged or about right.
- [ ] "What would change our view" lists concrete, checkable thresholds.
- [ ] The stock-market disclaimer is clear and not overbearing.
- [ ] **Q3 Where should investors look?** The sector shortlist logic (Supportive / Relatively resilient) is understandable. "Key risk and what would change this" opens and reads well.
- [ ] It is unmistakable that the economic backdrop is not an investment rating.
- [ ] Overall length and order: is anything below the three questions (indicator cards, "What changed?", "What is uncertain?") redundant or worth moving to a research page?

## 2. Economic conclusions — sanity check against your own judgment
- [ ] Does "Expanding, Firming, Moderate" match your read of the latest data (IP +2.2%, unemployment 4.1% flat, CPI ~3.7%)?
- [ ] Is anything important missing for an ordinary reader (for example interest rates, GDP, financial conditions)? Note it as a future-assignment candidate; don't change the model now.
- [ ] Monthly Reports page: the official assessment date and "What changed" make sense.

## 3. Historical research
- [ ] Historical page: the scorecard intro makes clear this is a retrospective check, "not a measure of forecasting accuracy and not a probability".

- [ ] False alarms (2003, 2016, 2023, 2024) are presented honestly.
- [ ] Direction table: the two outcome tests and base rates are understandable. Firming is shown as "not informative".
- [ ] "Scorecard limitations" disclosure opens and is complete.
- [ ] Links to the V5 historical research archive still work and are clearly marked as archive.
