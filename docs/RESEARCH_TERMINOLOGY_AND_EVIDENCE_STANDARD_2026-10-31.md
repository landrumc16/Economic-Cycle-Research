# Research terminology and evidence standard

Status: MANDATORY for future analytical outputs and historical revisions.
Date: 2026-10-31

## Principle
Use measurable observations, explicit definitions, dated sources, and reproducible classification rules. No event-, administration-, party-, or policy-specific exceptions. All hypotheses remain falsifiable. The same standards apply to endogenous and external-shock events.

## Prohibited as analytical labels
Avoid "soft landing", "hard landing", "healthy economy", "overheated market", "successful intervention", "failed intervention", "recession avoided", "recession postponed", "inevitable crash" and similar interpretive shorthand in scorecards, outcome fields and conclusions unless explicitly identified as a quotation or hypothesis.

## Standard measurements
- Recession: specified NBER peak/trough dates, or separately labeled mechanical GDP definition. Never conflate definitions.
- Equity drawdown: index, price/total-return convention, prior peak, threshold (e.g. -20%), first crossing date, horizon and recovery date.
- Output: GDP growth rate, vintage, release date and reference period.
- Labor: unemployment level/change, participation and payroll measures, vintage and reference period.
- Inflation: index, annual or monthly transformation, reference period, release date and vintage.
- Policy: instrument, decision date, implementation date, amount/rate and documented rationale; do not infer intent from election timing.
- Vulnerability: specified valuation ratio, credit spread, leverage ratio, funding stress, market breadth or other directly observed indicator, with threshold and calibration sample.
- Intervention outcome: before/after measures with explicit horizon; causal attribution requires separate design.
- Time to later recession: elapsed months, not proof of postponement or causal continuity.

## Universal event protocol
1. Apply the same outcome definition and inclusion thresholds to every historical period.
2. Record all qualifying episodes, including pandemic and other external shocks, in the master registry.
3. For mechanism-specific analyses, apply predeclared cohort rules uniformly and report exclusions and sensitivity results; no ad hoc exceptions.
4. Label uncertain causes as uncertain. Never force binary classification without evidence.
5. Record missing data and right-censoring explicitly; missing events are not verified non-events.
6. Distinguish observation, descriptive association, hypothesis and causal conclusion.
7. Maintain a provenance trail, source release/vintage and revision history.
8. Preserve competing explanations and counterexamples. Do not assume a recession must occur within any chosen horizon.

## Historical cleanup
Prior documents containing interpretive language remain archival and are not silently rewritten. New scorecards and dashboards must use the terminology above. Historical records should be migrated with explicit mappings and a documented review.

## Behavioral cycles and market adjustment
- Treat recurring market adjustments as observed historical phenomena, not evidence of a deterministic crash date or magnitude.
- Measure speculative positioning, leverage, concentration, risk appetite, liquidity, realized drawdowns and recoveries where historical data permit.
- Describe policy responses by instrument, timing and subsequent observations. Do not code interventions as having \"stopped\", \"prevented\", or \"postponed\" a crash without a separately identified causal counterfactual.
- A later decline does not establish continuity with an earlier imbalance; test persistence of the same vulnerabilities and competing initiating mechanisms.
- Distinguish any decline, a predeclared correction threshold (for example 10%), a predeclared bear-market threshold (for example 20%), and a recession; thresholds are conventions, not physical laws.
- Apply the same inclusion, censoring and classification rules to all episodes, including exogenous shocks. Mechanism-specific cohort exclusions must be declared in advance and shown alongside full-sample sensitivity.
