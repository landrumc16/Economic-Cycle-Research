# Economic Cycle Research — Version 6.1 Roadmap
**Status:** Approved direction; implementation pending.  
**Mission:** Conclusions first. Supporting evidence second. Detailed research third.  
**Baseline:** Version 6.0 is deployed; reuse its daily data pipeline, monthly research, sector inventory, source provenance, research archive and GitHub Pages publication. Do not rebuild.

## Definition of finished
A layperson can open the mobile homepage and answer in under one minute:
1. **Where are we?** The best-supported economic-cycle interpretation, or an explicit inconclusive result.
2. **Where are we heading?** Improving, weakening or mixed direction, with historical context, contrary evidence and uncertainty.
3. **Where should investors look?** Evidence-qualified sector outlooks across all 11 GICS sectors, distinguishing historical economic fit from present investment attractiveness.

Every claim must trace to dated sources. No invented cycle stage, probabilities, sector rankings, returns, allocation instructions, or false certainty. If evidence is insufficient, say so plainly and explain the gap.

## Delivery sequence
### 1. Research-to-conclusion contract
- Inventory existing V6 monthly assessment, historical analogues, mechanisms, negative controls and sector evidence.
- Build a structured, dated interpretation artifact with: headline, observed conditions, candidate phase, direction, historical analogues, disanalogies, confidence/uncertainty rationale, evidence references, missing-data disclosures and what would change the view.
- Reuse current metrics; keep current vintage and observation dates separate.
- **Acceptance:** deterministic tests for current, stale, contradictory, missing and revised inputs; no unsupported headline.

### 2. Historical cycle interpretation
- Define transparent phase criteria for expansion, slowing expansion, contraction and recovery using multiple domains, not one magic score.
- Compare multiple independent historical episodes and non-downturn controls. Track lead/lag ordering, overlap and dissimilarities.
- Separate observed direction from forecast; only present forward-looking claims when historical validation supports them. Otherwise say direction is unconfirmed.
- **Acceptance:** retrospective and holdout checks, episode independence, chronology and no-lookahead protections; interpretable evidence ledger.

### 3. Sector opportunity research
- Evaluate all 11 GICS sectors, with relevant industry differences.
- Separate **economic fit** (cycle sensitivity) from **investment fit** (earnings, valuations, balance sheets, benchmark-relative total returns).
- Require dated qualified sources, historical comparison samples, benchmark, counterexamples, survivorship and transaction-cost assumptions before a Favorable/Mixed/Caution rating.
- Display Insufficient Evidence where necessary; do not silently infer buy signals from macro patterns.
- **Acceptance:** evidence-gated classifications and clear rationale; all 11 sectors accounted for; tests prevent unqualified recommendations.

### 4. Executive homepage
- Mobile-first sections in this order: **Where We Are**, **Where We're Heading**, **Where Investors Should Look**.
- Plain-language 2–3 sentence conclusions, concise reasons, historical comparisons and prominent dates/uncertainty; avoid leading with raw statistics.
- Clearly mark inconclusive results, and link each conclusion to its detailed evidence.
- **Acceptance:** readable at phone width, accessible, accurate with missing/stale data, and smoke-tested against generated pages.

### 5. Research deep dives
- Organize collapsible detailed evidence: dated indicators, historical episodes, competing explanations, sector/industry analyses, methodologies, data revisions, prior monthly assessments.
- Keep research and technical caveats discoverable without overwhelming the homepage.
- **Acceptance:** navigation/link verification and claim-to-source traceability.

### 6. Release and verification
- Preserve one daily acquisition workflow and one official monthly assessment. Do not introduce a competing weekly pipeline.
- Run all Python/regression tests, generated-page checks, and publication gates; ensure failed updates retain the last accepted site.
- Commit implementation in substantial coherent chunks, deploy via existing GitHub Actions and verify the public URLs, including all 11 sector pages.
- **Acceptance:** successful monthly and publication runs, live GitHub Pages checks, and evidence-limited conclusions displayed honestly.

## Implementation rules
- Focus only on 6.1 until deployed; no V7, no unrelated refactors, no repeated roadmap planning.
- Follow small, simple, testable interfaces; reuse working components; batch coherent work and avoid asking for trivial decisions.
- Never turn historical similarity into a crash probability or historical sector performance into a personalized investment instruction.
- Preserve the source data, research methodology, and uncertainty beneath every readable headline.
