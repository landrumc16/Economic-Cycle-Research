## 1. The rules being reviewed

Code: `scripts/v63_interpretation.py`. The rules are published to readers at `research.html#rubric`.

| Input | Series | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Industrial production (INDPRO), 12-month log change | ≥ +0.5% expanding; ≤ −0.5% contracting; else flat |
| Growth momentum | Change in that 12-month rate over 3 months | ±0.5 pt → strengthening / weakening; else steady |
| Jobs | Unemployment rate (UNRATE), 3-month change | ≥ +0.3 pt deteriorating; ≤ −0.2 pt improving; else stable |
| Jobs warning | 3-month average unemployment vs its minimum over the prior 12 months | ≥ +0.5 pt → deteriorating (Sahm-style) |
| Inflation level | CPI (CPIAUCSL), 12-month log change | ≤ 2.5% near goal; > 4% high; else elevated |
| Inflation trend | Change in the CPI rate over 3 months | ±0.3 pt → firming / easing |
| Freshness | All three core series | ≤ 2 months old, from a verified https source, never later than the assessment date |

**Condition** (growth × jobs): expanding + steady/improving → **Expanding**; flat + steady/improving → **Slowing**; flat or contracting + deteriorating → **Deteriorating**; anything else → **Mixed**. Any stale, missing, unverified or future-dated core indicator means no call is made.

**Direction:** Weakening if jobs are deteriorating; otherwise Softening, Steady, Firming or Strengthening from production momentum and jobs; Unconfirmed when momentum is missing.

**Evidence confidence** = High / Moderate / Low, set by counting contrary signals, revisions to the same observation month, and genuinely missing evidence: 0 → High, 1–2 → Moderate, 3 or more → Low. It never expresses the probability of an event. Dating notes (for example, "September used because October's year-earlier value was never published") are shown but not penalised.

**Reversal conditions** are the same published thresholds, stated per condition.

## 3. Historical replay (real data, revised vintage)

Code: `scripts/v63_historical_replay.py`.

**Method:**
- For each month M from 1981-02 to 2026-10, the rules see only observations through M−1 (a one-month release-lag emulation).
- Outcomes are scored afterwards, using later data and NBER dates. Those dates are used only for scoring, never to produce a signal.
- A test confirms that changing same-month or later data does not change the signal.

**Months replayed:** 547.

**Recession timing.** A "warning" means the Deteriorating condition.

| NBER peak | Result | First warning | Months vs peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1980-01 | Not evaluable (coverage starts 1981-02) | — | — |
| 1981-07 | Not evaluable (needs the 12 months before the peak) | — | — |
| 1990-07 | Confirmation after peak | 1990-12 | 5 after |
| 2001-03 | Early warning | 2001-02 | 1 before |
| 2007-12 | Confirmation after peak | 2008-05 | 5 after |
| 2020-02 (pandemic, reported separately) | Confirmation after peak | 2020-04 | 2 after |

None of the 3 evaluable non-pandemic recessions was missed. Only one was flagged before its peak.

**False alarms:** 4 of 11 warning episodes (36%). A false alarm is a warning not followed within 12 months by a recession start, and with no rise of 0.5 points or more in unemployment within 6 months.
- 2003-07 to 2003-09 (3 months): jobless recovery
- 2016-05 (1 month): industrial slump driven by energy
- 2023-11 (1 month)
- 2024-08 to 2024-10 (3 months): the widely reported Sahm-rule trigger without a recession

**Direction calls versus the next six months, excluding the 2019-08 to 2021-06 pandemic window.**

Two different outcome tests are used:

| Group | Directions | Counted as right if… | Base rate (all evaluable months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cautious | Weakening, Softening | unemployment rose ≥ 0.2 pt **or** 12-month production growth fell ≥ 1 pt within 6 months | 54% |
| Constructive | Steady, Firming, Strengthening | unemployment did **not** rise ≥ 0.2 pt within 6 months (production ignored) | 65% |

Why the definitions matter:
- **The tests are not complements.** A month in which production fell ≥ 1 pt but unemployment rose less than 0.2 pt passes both, which is why the two base rates sum to more than 100%.
- **The cautious test is broader**, because either labor or production deterioration counts. It is easier to pass, and its base rate is lower only because deterioration is less common than calm.
- **Each share should be compared only with its own base rate.** Comparing a cautious share with a constructive share, or treating any share as an accuracy rate, is not valid.
- "Lift" is share minus base rate. A lift below 5 pt is labelled "not informative" (a labelling threshold, not a significance test).
- Months are overlapping and serially correlated, so there are far fewer independent observations than the month counts suggest. No confidence intervals are claimed.
- Unconfirmed months (momentum unavailable) and months whose 6-month outcome is not yet observable are excluded.
- These definitions were fixed before the real-data run. They were not changed after seeing the results.

## 4. Questions for the reviewer, with our own assessment

| Question | Evidence | Our assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Are the thresholds historically defensible? | §3 | Partly. Deteriorating is mostly a **confirmation** signal (5 months after the peak in 1990 and 2007), not an early warning. It caught every evaluable recession, with 4 false alarms in 45 years. The ±0.5 growth band and the 0.3 / 0.5 labor thresholds were chosen in advance from conventional practice (Sahm), not fitted. They have not been tuned, and tuning them on this same sample would overfit. |
| Does it find deterioration without excessive false alarms? | 4 of 11 episodes | False alarms are short (1–3 months) and two of them are well-documented labor-market quirks. We consider this acceptable for a descriptive monitor, provided the site keeps saying a warning is "not proof of recession". |
| Do confidence labels describe evidence quality, not probability? | `interpret()`; homepage wording | Yes by construction: the label is a count of contrary signals, revisions and missing inputs, and the page says "not the odds of any event". The thresholds that map the count to a label (0 / 1–2 / ≥ 3) are a design choice, not calibrated. |
| Does the replay contain lookahead or revised-data limitations? | Code and tests | No signal uses same-month or later observations (tested). **Revised-data bias remains**: current-vintage data is smoother than what was known in real time, so real-time false alarms and delays would probably have been worse. NBER dates are used only to score. Publication lags are emulated, not certified. |
| Do sector conclusions follow the same rules? | `economic_backdrop()` | Yes. The sector backdrop is a fixed lookup from the rubric condition and each sector's stated cycle sensitivity, plus an inflation note. It is labelled "not an investment rating". Investment ratings stay Insufficient Evidence for all 11 sectors because no qualified valuation, earnings or relative-return source exists. No such evidence has been created. |
| Are conclusions clear and decisive enough? | Homepage | The homepage states one condition, one direction, why, what argues against it, and what would change it. The main weakness is that "Expanding" with Moderate confidence can read as reassuring even though the rules confirm downturns late. The historical page says so explicitly. |

## 5. Known limitations the reviewer should weigh

1. Revised (current-vintage) data. No archived real-time vintages (ALFRED) are used.
2. Few recessions: 3 evaluable non-pandemic cases. The timing results are anecdotal.
3. Industrial production is a narrow growth measure (a manufacturing-heavy share of GDP).
4. Overlapping months are not independent, so the agreement shares are descriptive, not accuracy rates.
5. October 2025 was not published for CPI or UNRATE. As a result:
   - the replay skips months that need it;
   - the prior-year unemployment check stays unavailable until January 2027 data, when October 2025 drops out of its 15-month window. This is shown as an evidence gap.
6. Sector sensitivities summarise commonly cited research and have not been validated with sector returns.
