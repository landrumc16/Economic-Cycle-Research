# Behavioral foundation of market-cycle research

Status: foundational causal hypothesis; individual mechanisms require empirical validation.

## Core framing
Financial markets emerge from human decisions, incentives, beliefs, institutions and constraints. Study positive and negative feedback between expectations, prices, credit and liquidity. Do not assume every rising market is a bubble or every later decline proves an earlier crash was deferred.

## Candidate mechanisms and observables
- Extrapolative expectations / momentum chasing: return autocorrelation, valuation expansion, trading volume, flows.
- Social imitation / FOMO: investor participation, flows, issuance, concentration and survey evidence.
- Risk appetite / overconfidence: margin debt, leverage, credit terms, speculative issuance.
- Loss aversion / liquidity spirals: volatility, funding stress, deleveraging, market breadth and forced selling.
- Institutional incentives / policy expectations: documented actions, funding conditions, risk transfer and incentive structures.

## Empirical requirements
1. Define bubble criteria independently of subsequent crashes; avoid retrospective labeling based on outcomes.
2. Measure expectations with surveys or documented contemporaneous evidence when available; proxies alone do not establish motives.
3. Separate behavior, balance-sheet vulnerability, policy response, trigger and outcome in data models.
4. Evaluate both crash and non-crash episodes, including prolonged elevated valuations and partial corrections.
5. Keep recession and equity drawdown outcomes separate; compare 10% and 20% thresholds with explicit index and observation horizon.
6. Classify external initiating shocks consistently, retain them in the master record and report subgroup sensitivities.
7. Test whether vulnerability accumulation predicts greater frequency or severity of later adjustments without presuming deterministic inevitability.
8. Require original release vintages, complete outcome panels and independent holdouts before predictive claims.

## Research sequence
Beliefs/incentives -> positioning and risk-taking -> price and credit feedback -> balance-sheet vulnerability -> triggers and policy responses -> realized drawdowns, deleveraging and recovery.

## Next dataset increment
Create a period-level behavioral evidence matrix with dated price/valuation, issuance, leverage, credit, participation, surveys and funding data; tag each as direct observation or indirect proxy. Document missing fields instead of inferring sentiment.
